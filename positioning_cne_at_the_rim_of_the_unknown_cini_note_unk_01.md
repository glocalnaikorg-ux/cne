## 1. Early Provenance (circa 1991): The Ascent Toward the Unknown

Early thoughts on this question—dating back to around 1991—charted a deliberate ascent rather than a claim of final mastery:

> **The Unknown**  
> Higher than Contempt is Knowledge  
> Higher than Knowledge is Wisdom  
> Higher than Wisdom is Compassion  
> Higher than Compassion is the Unknown  

Carrying this 1991 sequence forward into **CNE** establishes why **Compassion** stands above **Knowledge** and **Wisdom**, yet still stops short of claiming **The Unknown**.

---

## 2. Putting the Record Straight: Not Yet Claimed

A vast amount of dedicated, well-reasoned, and thoroughly examined inquiry must go in before firmly claiming to dip into the Unknown without claiming to know one bit of it. **At this stage, CNE has not done this.**

This note places that restraint explicitly on the public record. Treating a profound horizon as an accomplished achievement before doing the long, disciplined work it demands would turn epistemic humility into a premature claim. For now, this posture stands as an open question and directional horizon rather than a completed attainment.

---

## 3. Illustrative Provenance Memory: BBC Observer-Intention Broadcast

Decades ago, a BBC television broadcast presented an experimental setup in which a human observer was asked simply to *will* or intend a falling object to deflect toward the right or toward the left. Across repeated trials, the object did not obey every command—there was no deterministic override of physical law—yet on a statistical basis, the outcomes drifted slightly more often in the direction willed by the observer than pure chance would predict. Crucially, the broadcast offered no speculative explanation; it presented only the observed facts.

Within CNE, this memory serves purely as an illustrative provenance reference:
* It reminds readers to separate raw empirical observation from invented dogma.
* Whether subtle statistical shifts reflect unmodeled biases, selection effects, or still-unexplained aspects of observation and measurement remains open—and CNE claims no ownership over an explanation.

## 4. Human Finitude as the Answer to Exclusivity

A common critique of speaking about becoming *The Compassionate* is the assumption that such a state is an elevated summit reachable by perhaps only one extraordinary figure rather than ordinary mortals.

The answer rests on human finitude: **a limited mind is not equipped to know the Unknown.** Precisely because every mortal mind is finite, no solitary individual and no exclusive hierarchy can claim complete mastery over the Unknown. Shared limitation prevents spiritual elitism and makes restraint, structural mercy, and care accessible to every mortal in **Nowness**.

---

## 5. Practical Contradiction and the Pre-Scientific-Revolution Hunter

Our position at the rim of the Unknown is akin to the successful hunter prior to the Scientific Revolution—for whom whether the Earth moves or is stationary is immaterial to tracking game and sustaining life. One can be right for the wrong—or incomplete—reasons.

Across science, politics, commerce, and daily existence, humanity was and remains a practical species. Our innate ability to function inside contradiction is rarely noted: we operate effectively in **Nowness** without possessing complete foundational truth. Recognizing this quiet capacity frees us from pretending to own ultimate answers before acting with restraint and care.

---

## 6. Great Multitudes and Doable Distractions

Just as the heavy foundational work has been done and handed forward by the **Past Great Multitudes**, serious, unhurried reflection by the **Present Great Multitudes** is needed to solidify our current understanding of this question.

Along the way, a great number of concrete, solvable, and **doable** tasks arise. Practical actions have their proper place in daily life and civic stewardship, yet they do not alter the boundary of the Unknown—which remains as unreachable as ever. Keeping that boundary clear helps readers position CNE accurately: grounded in **Nowness**, respectful of what has been inherited, and honest about what remains beyond our reach.
