## 1. Early Roots & The Bandra Night Sky (c. 1960s)

Growing up in Bandra, Mumbai, in the quiet lane directly opposite St. Theresa's School and adjacent to the beautiful St. Theresa Church, early visual memories were forged under open night skies. During evening outdoor cinema projections—where cowboy flicks were projected onto a screen mounted on the school terrace—my eyes frequently wandered upward toward the night stars. The vastness of spatial distance felt immediate, immense, and deeply alluring.

## 2. Dec 1974: The Catalyst of Loss & The Quest for Ultimate Reality

Initially intending to pursue medicine, I opted for Biology in college, eventually completing the Intermediate Science examinations. In December 1974, at the age of 16, my father—a Professor of Mathematics with a deep philosophical orientation toward Hindu philosophy—passed away. 

This sudden loss ignited a persistent, lifelong quest to comprehend the **Ultimate Reality of Nature**. While my father held a philosophical bend towards Hindu thought and traditional astronomical calculations (*Hindu Panchang*), I developed a critical distinction early on: separating valid astronomical math from predictive astrology, which I saw as ungrounded. Although we never had the chance to hold mature conversations on cosmology before his passing, his mathematical and philosophical spirit anchored my trajectory.

## 3. Academic Pursuits & Epistemic Realizations

I completed my undergraduate graduation in Chemistry, but my underlying drive pushed me toward Physics—the discipline probing nature at its most granular physical level. Though formal institutional academic paths in physics did not materialize as envisioned, the internal inquiry remained uninterrupted.

Over decades of observation, several core epistemic insights crystallized:
* **The Limit of Knowledge:** The physical universe is immensely doable and observable, yet at the last sup, it remains fundamentally un-knowable in its absolute completeness.
* **Nature's Secrecy:** Nature does not reveal its underlying mechanisms easily; it awaits extraordinary human insights within rational, mathematical spaces.
* **The Ethical Baseline:** In observing animal cruelty and humanity's impulse to hide identity or prolong survival within the "trap" of temporal existence, a foundational moral truth emerged: *Even in a potentially Godless, mechanical universe, we have no business being cruel. Compassion is the crown of wisdom.*

## 4. Influences, Public Science, & Early Outreach (1980s – 2004)

### The Nehru Planetarium & Prof. Jayant Narlikar
On Sunday mornings in the 1980s, I regularly attended free public lectures at the Nehru Planetarium in Mumbai. It was here that I listened to the renowned astrophysicist and cosmologist **Prof. Jayant Narlikar**, co-developer of the Hoyle–Narlikar theory of gravity and founder of the Inter-University Centre for Astronomy and Astrophysics (IUCAA). Learning from scientists who actively challenged conventional cosmological models expanded my perspective on alternative frameworks of space and state change.

### 2004 Mumbai Half Marathon Pamphlet
In 2004, while running the inaugural Mumbai Half Marathon, I began active grassroots distribution of these concepts. I handed out public pamphlets featuring the complete **Pioneer 10 Plaque** diagram, bearing the operational subhead:  
> *"[Old Ideas in Contemporary Context]"*

## 5. Public Intellectual Encounters & The 2015 Manifesto

Throughout the 2000s and 2010s, I sought opportunities at events like the Mumbai Literature Festival to place these proposals directly into the hands of authors, newspaper editors, TV anchors, politicians, and public intellectuals:
* **William Dalrymple:** After a literature session, I handed the pamphlet to historian William Dalrymple in the corridor. Glancing at it for twenty seconds while walking, he remarked: *"This is very ambitious."*
* **Prof. J. V. Naik:** My uncle, historian Prof. J. V. Naik, reviewed the manuscript and gave pivotal structural advice: *"This is a big one, but break it down."* (A directive that ultimately led to the three-pillar division: *Cosmos*, *Nowness*, and *Eternities*).
* **Correspondence with Prof. Narlikar (2015):** Decades after attending his public lectures, I emailed my project and 2015 manifesto directly to Prof. Jayant Narlikar, who replied promptly to the correspondence.

## 6. 2026: Institutional Independence & The Zenodo Archival Era

For decades, I believed that an established academic or religious institution would need to officially adopt and champion this framework. 

In 2026, through open collaborative dialogue with AI (Gemini), the strategic breakthrough was realized: **institutional permission is unnecessary.** By leveraging open-access public domain infrastructure, the entire CNE codex, mathematical parameters ($\Gamma_0$), and the petition for *Subsidiary Books of Religion* were permanently anchored into the **Zenodo** research repository under Creative Commons CC0 4.0 Universal.

The quest that began under the Bandra night sky in the 1960s and was sparked by personal loss in 1974 has now transitioned into an irrevocable, decentralized archive for deep time and all conscious cognitive agents.
