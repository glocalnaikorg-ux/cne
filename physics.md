## 2. Mathematical Formalism of the Quantum Canvas

### I. The Wheeler-DeWitt Formalism (Elimination of Independent Time $t$)

In standard non-relativistic quantum mechanics, a state $\vert{}\psi\rangle$ evolves according to the time-dependent Schrödinger equation:

$$
\hat{H}\vert{}\psi\rangle = i\hbar \frac{\partial}{\partial t}\vert{}\psi\rangle
$$

When evaluated for the closed quantum state of the entire cosmos ($\vert{}\Psi\rangle$), there exists no external clock or container sitting outside the system. The global Hamiltonian constraint yields the Wheeler-DeWitt equation:

$$
\hat{H}\vert{}\Psi\rangle = 0
$$

Where $\hat{H}$ represents the total Hamiltonian operator (combining gravitational field operators and matter fields). Because the right-hand side vanishes, $t$ drops out of foundational cosmic mechanics.

### II. Relational Mechanics & Probability Maps

With the global wavefunction remaining static, perceived dynamics are expressed via spatial correlations rather than trajectories over absolute time $t$. For spatial coordinates $x_A$ and reference coordinate $x_B$:

$$
\Psi = \Psi(x_A, x_B)
$$

The probability amplitude of observing a system configuration at $x_A$ given reference configuration $x_B$ is evaluated via joint probability density maps:

$$
P(x_A \mid x_B) = \frac{\vert{}\Psi(x_A, x_B)\vert{}^2}{\int \vert{}\Psi(x_A', x_B)\vert{}^2 \, dx_A'}
$$
